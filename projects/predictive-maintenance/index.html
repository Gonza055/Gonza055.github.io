    Sensor Data to Reliability

    Maintenance data analysis to understand equipment behavior and build a stronger analytical foundation for condition monitoring and future predictive maintenance.

    Sensor Data Time-Series Analysis Reliability Analytics Asset Context

    Context

    Maintenance data in a real mining operation

    During my internship at Compañía de Minas Buenaventura, I worked with field sensor data from critical equipment to analyze operating behavior and create a reliable data foundation for condition monitoring and future predictive-maintenance work.

    RoleMaintenance Data Analyst Intern
    OrganizationCompañía de Minas Buenaventura
    LocationSan Gabriel · Peru
    Period2025
    Main focusSensor data · reliability analysis

    The Problem

    The data existed, but it was not analysis-ready.

    01

    Missing data
    Gaps and inconsistencies reduced trust in the raw histories.

    02

    Noise and outliers
    Sensor noise and abnormal values could dominate the analysis.

    03

    Limited context
    Equipment, maintenance, and process context needed to be connected.

    04

    Not analysis-ready
    Signals required conditioning, validation, and structured engineering logic.

    Outcome

    A stronger foundation for predictive maintenance.

    The work produced cleaner sensor histories, reusable reliability features, more structured asset information, and a clearer basis for condition monitoring and later predictive-maintenance prototyping.

    This was not a production-ready failure-prediction model. It was the engineering and analytical foundation needed before one could be developed responsibly.

    What I Learned

    Predictive maintenance starts before the prediction.

    A reliable model depends on good data, engineering understanding, and clear context. Signal conditioning, asset structure, process behavior, and domain interpretation determine whether predictive analysis can eventually become useful.
