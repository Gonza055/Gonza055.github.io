    Context

    Real maintenance analytics work at San Gabriel.

    HPGR roller wear & operational monitoring

    During my Buenaventura internship, I analyzed HPGR roller-wear histories together with production, material, and operational sensor data. The goal was to understand degradation patterns, identify data-quality limitations, and determine what information could responsibly support condition monitoring and later predictive-maintenance work.

    The Data Problem

    The challenge was not just modeling — it was making the data trustworthy enough to analyze.

    01

    Temporal misalignment
    Wear histories and available sensor data did not fully overlap in time, so direct sensor-to-wear correlations could not be forced responsibly.

    02

    Sensor anomalies
    Temperature values around 6528°C clearly indicated calibration or recording errors that had to be identified before analysis.

    03

    Missing / inconsistent records
    Material fields contained nulls, campaign counters reset, and comparison sheets mixed measured and projected values.

    04

    Multiple operating campaigns
    Four alternating Vulco/Weir and Hofmann campaigns required a consistent basis for comparison.

    Technical Evidence

    Rebuilt directly from the HPGR workbook and report findings.

    Wear is highly structured within each campaign.

    The workbook shows a repeated pattern: roller thickness declines as accumulated tonnage increases, then resets when a new campaign begins. This makes campaign-aware wear estimation possible, while still requiring engineering context before using it as a maintenance rule.

    Global wear signal

    The complete dataset shows a moderate inverse relationship between thickness and accumulated tonnage, with the report documenting r ≈ -0.63 and a global linear model with R² ≈ 0.39.

    Material and operating context matter

    MAT 2 showed a strong inverse association with thickness (r ≈ -0.81). It was treated as an investigation signal — not a causal conclusion — because material definitions and synchronized operating context still needed engineering validation.

    Outcome

    A defensible analytical foundation — not a premature failure model.

    01Campaign wear patterns became measurable. Thickness and tonnage could be compared on a consistent basis.
    02Data-quality risks were made explicit. Impossible sensor values and missing records were documented instead of hidden.
    03Potential drivers were identified. Tonnage and material mix became candidates for deeper engineering analysis.
    04Predictive work was scoped more responsibly. Better temporal overlap and cleaner sensor histories were identified as prerequisites.
    This was not a production-ready failure-prediction model. The work established the exploratory, data-quality, and reliability foundations needed before one could be developed responsibly.

    What I Learned

    Predictive maintenance starts before the prediction.

    The strongest lesson was that modeling is only one layer of the problem. Data quality, asset definitions, operating context, maintenance standards, and engineering judgment determine whether an analytical result can become useful.
