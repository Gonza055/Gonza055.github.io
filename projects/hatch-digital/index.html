    From Mining Data to Decisions.

    Three applied workstreams — Drones/CAEX, HITM, and SIC — showed me the same lesson from different angles: digital work starts with the operational problem, not with the technology.

    RoleDigital Student

    ContextMining & industrial operations

    Context

    Digital work in a real mining context.

    This internship was not about technology in the abstract. Each workstream began with a practical question: what decision are we trying to improve, what information does that decision require, and who can validate that the result is trustworthy?

    The projects were different in scope and maturity, but my way of contributing repeated: investigate, structure, develop, and validate with engineers, analysts, and domain specialists.

    The recurring principle

    Observe the context, structure the problem, build or investigate the right tool, and validate the result with the people who understand the operation.

    The Work

    Three workstreams. Three different questions.

    The technology changed from project to project. The standard for usefulness did not: the output had to be understandable, reviewable, and connected to a real decision.

    01 · Drones / CAEX

    Can a drone measurement be trusted?

    I researched and compared capture and reconstruction alternatives, then translated them into criteria for evaluating whether a drone-based payload measurement could be technically defensible.

    Scope: conceptual investigation and viability assessment — not implementation or operational deployment.

    02 · HITM

    Automation without losing engineering logic.

    I supported the transformation of heterogeneous technical sources into consistent, reviewable outputs for Hatch Integrated Tailings Management while preserving rules, exceptions, and technical validation.

    Workflow: receive → prepare → validate → generate output.

    03 · SIC

    Decide during the shift, not after it.

    I helped structure the operating model for how a deviation becomes a traceable decision: define the event, assess impact and priority, assign ownership, act, close the loop, and retain the learning.

    My focus: events, levels, roles, minimum data, thresholds, responsibility, and closure logic.

    One Pattern

    Different tools. The same way of thinking.

    Across Drones, HITM, and SIC, the work repeatedly moved through the same four stages.

    Understand the systemStart with the operation, constraints, and the decision that needs to improve.
    Structure the informationDefine inputs, context, rules, exceptions, and handoffs.
    Validate the resultMake technical review explicit and identify who can confirm that the output is trustworthy.
    Support the decisionUse software, automation, or analytics only where they enable a clearer operational response.

    Three Questions Before Building

    Technology comes after the problem is defined.

    These three questions became a simple way to start digital work before choosing a tool or architecture.

    1 · DecisionWhat decision are we trying to improve?
    2 · InformationWhat data, structure, rules, context, and exceptions does that decision require?
    3 · ValidationWho can confirm that the result is reliable and useful in the operation?

    Evidence

    The technical work also had to be explained and reviewed.

    Presenting the logic, discussing assumptions, and receiving feedback from the team were part of the work — not an afterthought.

    Outcomes

    What each workstream contributed.

    Drones / CAEX

    Converted an open measurement question into a structured set of viability and confidence criteria.

    HITM

    Helped make technical data preparation more consistent, reviewable, and explicit about rules and exceptions.

    SIC

    Helped frame a decision cycle around events, impact, roles, thresholds, ownership, and closure.

    Across the internship

    Built experience translating ambiguous operational problems into structured digital work with specialist review.

    What I Learned

    The code is only one part of the work.

    My main takeaway from Hatch Digital was that useful digital work sits between the operation, the data, and the software. Programming matters, but so do good questions, explicit assumptions, technical validation, and understanding who will use the result.
